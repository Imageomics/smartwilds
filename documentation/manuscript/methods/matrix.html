2.4 AI-assisted annotation pipeline

Each modality was processed using a domain-specific AI annotation pipeline (Table 1). All pipelines convert raw sensor output into the unified observation schema (Section 2.3); the comparison across modalities therefore rests on a common representation rather than on modality-specific formats. The pipeline is model-agnostic: each stage accepts any detection or classification backend that produces bounding boxes, species labels, and confidence scores, enabling straightforward replacement of individual models as the state of the art advances.

Camera traps

Images were first passed through MegaDetector v5 to separate animal detections from blank frames and to produce bounding-box crops. Crops were then classified to species using BioCLIP v2, a vision-language model trained on biological imagery. Detections below a confidence threshold of [XX] were flagged for manual review by the annotator (Bharath). SAM3 was used for instance segmentation on a subset of images to support morphological trait extraction and individual re-identification via coat-pattern comparison.

Depth estimation and GPS association

To move camera trap detections from site-level to local (metre-scale) spatial resolution, we applied monocular depth estimation to each annotated image. The detection bounding-box centroid was projected to an estimated ground distance using camera mounting height and tilt angle as calibration parameters. This estimated position was then associated with the nearest GPS collar fix within a [XX]-minute temporal window, assigning the detection a candidate individual identity and a georeferenced location. Depth estimation accuracy was evaluated against [XX] detections with known positions [from drone orthophoto / GPS ground truth], yielding a median positional error of [XX] m (IQR [XX]–[XX] m). Although imperfect, this precision is sufficient to assign detections to habitat patches at the spatial grain used in the occupancy analysis (Section 2.6). The pipeline accepts alternative depth estimation backends; we used [current SOTA model] for all results reported here.

ARUs

Audio files were processed with BirdNET (v2.4) to classify bird vocalizations in 3-second windows across each recording. Detections with confidence ≥ [XX] were retained. A subset of detections was also processed with Perch for cross-model comparison. Tanishka validated a stratified random sample of [XX] detections against spectrograms and, where available, concurrent observer logs.

Drone video

Frames were extracted at [XX] fps and passed through YOLO with SAHI (sliced inference) to detect animals at typical flight altitudes of [XX]–[XX] m. OWL-ViT was used for open-vocabulary detection of target species. Species classification of drone detections is more uncertain than camera trap classification owing to the overhead perspective and variable resolution; detections were assigned to species where confidence exceeded [XX] and otherwise retained at genus or functional-group level.

Citizen science

Photographs and videos collected by visiting researchers and students during field campaigns were manually annotated by Jenna to species level. All observations were georeferenced from device metadata or from deployment site coordinates where GPS was unavailable.

Text data

Unstructured and semi-structured text files (field notes, deployment logs, drone mission notes, JotForm CSVs, bioacoustic summaries) were processed using an LLM-based extraction pipeline to identify species mentions, habitat descriptions, and temporal references. Extracted records were linked to the unified schema by site ID and date. LLM extraction outputs were manually reviewed for false positives (hallucinated species) and false negatives (missed mentions) on a random sample of [XX] documents, achieving a precision of [XX] and recall of [XX].

2.5 Biodiversity metrics

For each modality and for multi-modal fusion, we computed three biodiversity metrics.

Species richness is the count of distinct species detected per modality, standardised using XX to account for differences in sampling effort.

Relative abundance is the count of individuals per species per unit effort (trap-nights for camera traps, recorder-hours for ARUs, flight-hours for drones, observer-hours for citizen science, and document-count for text data).

Land-use detection is the binary presence or absence of each species at each site aggregated over the deployment period.

2.6 Detection probability and recall against ground truth

The managed setting at The Wilds provides unusually strong ground truth for evaluating sensor recall. During the summer 2025 campaign, there were 101 Milu deer (44 males and 57 females), 40 Sichuan takin (19 males and 21 females), and 9 Przewalski's horses (1 male and 8 females) on site in the two pastured enclosures. These census counts were obtained from the park's animal management team and represent the total number of individuals of each species present during the deployment period.

We used these counts to estimate per-modality detection probability for managed ungulate species as:

p̂m  =  Ndetected,m Ncensus

where Ndetected,m is the number of distinct individuals detected by modality m (using depth-estimated GPS association for camera traps, visual re-identification for drones, and group count for ARUs and citizen science) and Ncensus is the census count for that species.

Note that p̂m is a lower bound on true detection probability because (a) not all individuals have equal probability of appearing in sensor coverage areas and (b) individual re-identification from camera traps and drones is imperfect. We report p̂m with this caveat and compare it across modalities to quantify the marginal gain from fusion. For the fused estimate we use the union of individuals detected across all modalities. We do not apply this analysis to bird species detected by ARUs or to species detected only in text data, where individual-level ground truth is unavailable.

2.7 Cross-modal complementarity analysis

Species overlap matrix

We constructed a modality × modality overlap matrix using the Jaccard similarity J(A,B) = |SA ∩ SB| / |SA ∪ SB| of detected species sets SA and SB for each pair of modalities. Low Jaccard values indicate high complementarity (each modality detects taxa the other misses). The matrix is visualised as a heatmap.

Species × modality detection matrix

A binary detection matrix (species × modality) was constructed for all species detected across the deployment. Rows are clustered by taxonomic group (mammals, birds, other); columns are ordered by modality. Taxa detected by only one modality are highlighted as uniquely detected species. This matrix is the primary empirical output of the study and directly populates the sensor × research-question framework.

Ablation analysis

To quantify the marginal contribution of each modality to total detected species richness, we performed an additive ablation: starting from the lowest-cost modality, we computed cumulative species richness as modalities are added in order of deployment cost. We also report the loss in richness when each modality is removed from the full six-modality set. This produces a diminishing-returns curve that is the empirical basis for the Pareto-optimal sensor recommendations in the Discussion.
