    Cross-modal coordination: planning a sensing system from its metadata¶

    Distributed sensing systems are expensive to deploy. A camera-trap network, a set of acoustic recorders, drone flights, and visitor photographs each cost field time, hardware, and compute, and once deployed they are hard to change. Yet the planning tools that other infrastructure takes for granted, the ones that tell you which hardware tier, which sensor configuration, which model, and which filtering threshold a deployment needs before you build it, do not exist for ecological sensing. Systems get sized by rule of thumb and corrected in the field.

    The metadata in this release is what makes such tools possible. Fine-grained timestamps, sensor configurations, and confidence-scored detections, aligned across modalities, are enough to characterize each modality's workload, to synthesize the workload a coordinated multi-modal system would produce, and to predict whether a given hardware tier can keep up. The paper A Blueprint for Cross-Modal Coordination at the Far Edge (Kline, Potlapally, Cheng, Berger-Wolf, Subramoni, Stewart; to appear) develops that method on the SmartWilds metadata, and analysis/cross_modal_coordination/ is its code and derived data, included here as a worked example of what the metadata enables.

    Paper abstract

    Multimodal autonomous systems deployed in challenging far-edge settings, such as precision agriculture, search-and-rescue, wildfire and disaster response, and ecological monitoring, collect heterogeneous data streams whose combined signal exceeds the sum of their parts. This opens an action space in which one modality's inference can direct another's sampling to concentrate scarce compute, bandwidth, and energy where the signal is richest. Realizing this cross-modal coordination at the edge, however, is a challenging coupled-provisioning problem: hardware, sensor configuration, model choice, and data-filtering thresholds each shape the joint workload and depend on it in turn, and the workload cannot be measured until a coordinated deployment exists, which is prohibitively costly to iterate in remote field settings. We propose a record-characterize-synthesize approach that probes such future systems by leveraging co-located, temporally aligned single-modality deployments as a proxy for the spatiotemporal conditions of a cross-modal deployment. We apply our approach to a 24-day multimodal field deployment whose preserved metadata schema (temporal alignment, spatial registration, and inferential confidence across all streams) supports cross-modal workload reasoning that existing multimodal benchmark datasets cannot. We report the findings from the workload characterization and synthesis, providing a blueprint for feasible cross-modal field deployments. This work scopes cross-modal coordination at the edge as a distinct systems domain and lays empirical groundwork for the next generation of mission-driven multi-modal autonomous system deployments.

    The method: record, characterize, synthesize¶

    flowchart LR
  R[Record<br/>co-located single-modality<br/>deployments] --> C[Characterize<br/>per-modality workload model<br/>rate, burstiness, co-occurrence, throughput]
  C --> S[Synthesize<br/>a coordinated multi-modal trace<br/>from a recipe and a seed]
  S --> P[Predict<br/>hardware tier x policy<br/>feasibility grid]
    Phase What it produces Where
    Characterize Workload model per modality: change-point arrival rates, burstiness, diurnal profile, cross-modal co-occurrence scripts/01_characterize/, shipped as data/primitives/
    Synthesize A synthetic multi-modal trace from a recipe: which modalities, how one modality's detections re-shape another's sampling (interleave, conditional scaling, prior injection), at what intensity scripts/02_synthesize/
    Case studies Four coordination scenarios: acoustic detection triggers cameras; a camera triggers a drone; a focal camera re-tunes its neighbors; citizen-science reports act as priors for everything scripts/03_case_studies/
    Fidelity Does synthesis stay within the natural variation of the recorded sites? Identity test, variance envelope, topology scaling, intensity sweep scripts/04_fidelity/
    Feasibility Predicted hardware tier × inference policy grid for each case study, calibrated against one measured cell scripts/05_feasibility/
    Figures and supplement The paper's figures and the tables cut from it scripts/figures/, supplement/

    Synthesis is deterministic: the same recipe and seed always produce the same trace. The identity recipe re-emits the recorded event counts within 0.1 to 4.3 percent for every modality.

    What it says about the metadata¶

    • Burstiness is a joint property of modality and deployment topology. Camera-trap and drone arrivals have burstiness above 0.5, so provisioning must be for peak rate, not mean rate, and adding sites changes burstiness in a modality-specific way. None of this is visible without second-level, corrected timestamps.
    • Cross-modal co-occurrence is low at the hour scale (all pairwise Jaccard below 0.05), which licenses treating modalities as independent in synthesis and is itself a finding about how these sensors sample the landscape.
    • Feasibility verdicts depend on sensor configuration. The acoustic recorder's schedule (hourly versus dawn-and-dusk chorus) changes the response tier a camera network needs; the camera's photo-versus-video mode changes the detection pipeline's load. The sensor configurations in metadata/sensor_configurations.md are inputs to the model, not footnotes.
    • A field laptop is the limiting tier under tight latency targets, and the one measured cell confirmed the prediction within 23 percent.

    Running it¶

    cd analysis/cross_modal_coordination
pip install -r requirements.txt
./run_all.sh                          # identity synthesis, four case studies, fidelity checks
for f in scripts/figures/fig_*.py; do python3 "$f"; done   # the paper's figures

    No archive access is needed: the shipped workload primitives are enough to synthesize all four modalities. Read REPRODUCIBILITY.md first; it lists what reproduces exactly, what needs the field archive, and which published numbers carry caveats. supplement/README.md holds the tables and figures cut from the camera-ready.

    Relation to the rest of the release¶

    The arrival statistics that feed this analysis are the same ones shipped with the study-level dataset (analysis/arrival_traces_v2/, see Arrival traces), and the workload primitives derive from the Darwin Core Occurrence tables documented here. The point of keeping that metadata clean and aligned is precisely that analyses like this one become possible.
